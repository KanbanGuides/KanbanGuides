# Kanban translation policy and review

The shared [OGP contributor guide](https://github.com/nkdAgility/OpenGuidePlatform/blob/v1.1.5/system/OpenGuidePlatform.Agents.Integration/contributor-guide/README.md) explains how to do the work. This page records KanbanGuides decisions and approval requirements. Start with the [site translation entry point](./translations.md).

## Agree Kanban editorial decisions

Contact the translation guardian through a site discussion or KanbanGuides Slack. Agree the language and regional variant, website scope, selected guide editions, glossary, team contacts and feedback arrangements. Do not assume every historical edition needs translating.

The [Translations Code of Conduct](./translations-code-of-conduct.md) governs the Open Guide to Kanban and associated materials; official translators must agree to it. Confirm the applicable policy for other guides. Follow its reserved-term and first-use English rules, references, licence requirements and acknowledgement eligibility. AI assistance is permitted under that policy, with human review and correction required.

Agree script-specific conventions with the guardian, including meaningful case or italics, adaptation markings, reading direction and fonts. Record unresolved choices. Keep private coordination details out of public PRs.

Record consent before sharing names, profile links or personal information. Structured translation credits do not replace the in-document Translator Acknowledgement or last-page change history; follow sections 4, 6 and 7 of the Code of Conduct.

## Kanban review and delivery

Keep new languages excluded from production and preserve supplied/protected PDFs. Include the agreed discussion, scope, source/candidate revisions, rendering evidence, consented credits and unresolved questions in the PR. OGP's shared guide explains how to collect this evidence.

The intended handoff is a maintainer-approved PR canary for technical/design review, followed by preview for native-speaker review. The [site entry point](./translations.md#local-validation-and-review-environments) records the current fork deployment limitation and local destinations. Review homepage/navigation layout, fonts, PDF shaping, covers and links on narrow and wide screens.

For the Open Guide to Kanban, the Code of Conduct requires at least five native-speaker reviewers, ideally three with Kanban knowledge and two without. Written, audio or video feedback is accepted as the policy allows. Track findings to resolution and inspect the final rendered wording.

The guardian approves publication after language review, resolved feedback and final HTML, Markdown and PDF checks. Maintainers handle production promotion separately. Retain useful glossary and review decisions, and notify the guardian if the team can no longer maintain the translation. Shared command or rendering defects belong in OGP; Kanban terminology, wrapper design and delivery arrangements belong here.
